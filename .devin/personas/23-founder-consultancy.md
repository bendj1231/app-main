# Persona 23 — Founder's Consultancy (building the app from scars)

## Profile
- **Hours**: 700 (Daniel Doyle's lived experience)
- **Licenses**: CPL, IR, CFI, ATR type rating, AMT, B737 AMT rating
- **Current role**: Founder of PilotRecognition.com
- **Situation**: Every feature is a scar. Every persona is a wound. Building the app is both redemption and therapy.
- **App context**: This IS the app. The founder's questions ARE the product roadmap.

## 100 Questions

### The Founder's Origin — Why This App Exists
1. I'm building this app because I was lied to — degree, hours, type rating, AMT. Every trap. Is that a foundation or a grudge?
2. Every feature in the app is a mistake I made — is that product design or autobiography?
3. The app exists because no one told me the truth — is that a mission statement or a trauma response?
4. I spent $200K and 8 years on aviation and walked away with nothing — the app is what I have to show for it. Is that enough?
5. The app is called "Pilot Recognition" — but I was never recognized. Am I building what I needed, or what I still want?
6. Every user persona is a version of me — student, CPL, CFI, type-rated, stuck, waiting, leaving. Is that empathy or narcissism?
7. I'm building the app I wish existed when I was 17 — is that the right product or just my therapy?
8. The app should prevent the next Daniel Doyle — but can an app do what no mentor, school, or airline did for me?
9. I left aviation and I'm building an aviation app — am I in aviation or am I outside it? Can you build for a community you've left?
10. The app is my redemption — if it saves one person from the ATR type rating trap, my $25K wasn't wasted. Is that rational or just hopeful?

### The Honesty Problem — Will the Industry Hate Me?
11. The app will expose flight schools with bad outcomes — will schools sue me for publishing their real employment rates?
12. The app will tell people NOT to self-fund type ratings — will training centers try to destroy me?
13. The app will show that the "pilot shortage" is a myth at entry level — will airlines discredit me?
14. The app will show real first-year FO pay ($31K, food stamps) — will airlines claim I'm "discouraging future pilots"?
15. The app will feature "career exit" pathways — will the aviation community call me a quitter who's dragging others down?
16. The app will show that CFI is not a sustainable career — will CFIs feel attacked or validated?
17. The app will expose the seniority trap — will airlines pressure me to remove that content?
18. The app will show international pilot outcomes (expat loneliness, divorce rates) — will Middle East carriers threaten legal action?
19. The app will tell 18-year-olds "maybe don't spend $80K without a plan" — will parents thank me or resent me?
20. The app will be the first honest voice in aviation — is the industry ready for honesty, or will it shoot the messenger?

### The Data Problem — How Do I Get the Truth?
21. The app needs real employment outcome data — schools won't share it. How do I get it?
22. The app needs real salary data — airlines don't publish first-year take-home. How do I get it?
23. The app needs real career transition rates — regionals to majors, CFI to airline, cargo to airline. Where does that data live?
24. The app needs real divorce/mental health data for pilots — who collects that? Does anyone?
25. The app needs real training center placement rates — they all claim 85%+. How do I verify?
26. The app should crowdsource pilot outcomes — "what happened to you?" survey. Will pilots share honestly?
27. The app should partner with pilot unions for data — ALPA, IPA, BALPA. Will they cooperate with a startup?
28. The app should scrape airline job postings for real minimums vs. posted minimums — is that legal? Useful?
29. The app should track "hours to first job" by school, region, and year — can I build that dataset from user logbooks?
30. The app's data is its moat — if I can collect real outcomes, no one can compete. But can I get enough users to make the data meaningful?

### The Monetization Problem — How Does This Make Money?
31. The app needs revenue — but charging pilots (who are broke) feels wrong. What's the business model?
32. The app could charge flight schools for listings — but then I can't expose bad schools. Conflict of interest?
33. The app could charge airlines for recruitment — but then I can't show real first-year pay. Conflict of interest?
34. The app could charge for premium features (logbook, exam prep, career coaching) — but pilots are the least-funded users. Will they pay?
35. The app could sell data to training centers — "here's what pilots want." But is that betraying the users?
36. The app could offer a "career consultation" service — paid sessions with experienced pilots. Is that scalable?
37. The app could run ads — but aviation ads are mostly flight schools selling the dream. Hypocritical?
38. The app could be a freemium model — free pathways and logbook, paid exam prep and coaching. What's the conversion rate?
39. The app could partner with lenders — "Get Funded" referrals. But I'd be sending people into debt. Ethical?
40. The app's monetization can't compromise its honesty — if I take money from schools or airlines, I become their marketing tool. How do I stay independent?

### The Product Roadmap — What Matters Most?
41. The app has 10 career categories, 30+ pathways, logbook, exam portal, type rating search — what's the priority?
42. The app's logbook is functional but basic — should I improve it or focus on career pathways?
43. The app's exam portal has categories — should I add ATPL theory practice? CFI practice? Type rating prep?
44. The app's "Get Hired" section lists airlines — should I add real hiring data or keep it informational?
45. The app's "Get Funded" section lists loans and scholarships — should I add a "financial reality" calculator?
46. The app's "Get Recognition" section has PRP and Atlas CV — should I make it the "LinkedIn for pilots"?
47. The app's mentor network — should I vet mentors? Rate them? Pay them? How does it scale?
48. The app's "pilot shortage" brand (pilotshortage.org) — should I rebrand it as "pilot shortage myth" or keep it?
49. The app's search system — should it search jobs, not just aircraft and airlines? Is it becoming a job board?
50. The app's roadmap should be driven by user pain — which persona's pain is most urgent? The student? The CFI? The FO? The retiree?

### The User Acquisition Problem — How Do I Get Pilots?
51. The app needs users — but pilots are skeptical of new platforms. How do I earn their trust?
52. The app should go viral on aviation TikTok/Instagram — but I need honest content, not "living the dream" content. Will honesty go viral?
53. The app should partner with aviation YouTubers — but they sell the dream. Will they promote a reality-check app?
54. The app should be discussed on r/flying and r/aviation — but Reddit can be hostile to startups. How do I engage authentically?
55. The app should target flight schools — but if I expose bad ones, good ones won't partner either. How do I position it?
56. The app should have a "tell your story" feature — pilots share their journey, anonymously if needed. Will they?
57. The app should have a referral program — "invite 3 pilots, get premium free." Does that work in aviation?
58. The app should be at aviation conferences — Oshkosh, Aero Friedrichshafen, Pilot Career Expo. Can I afford a booth?
59. The app should target the PARENTS of aspiring pilots — they're the ones paying. Do they search for "is flight school worth it"?
60. The app's user acquisition is its biggest challenge — build a great product that nobody uses, and it fails. How do I get the first 10,000 users?

### The Competitor Problem — Who Else Is Doing This?
61. Are there other apps doing what PilotRecognition does — career pathways, logbook, exam prep, all in one?
62. ForeFlight has logbooks — but not career pathways. Is that a competitor or a complement?
63. Pilot Career Centre, AviationCV — do they offer what I offer? Are they honest or just job boards?
64. CAE, L3Harris have training pathways — but they're selling their own training. Am I the independent alternative?
65. LinkedIn has pilot profiles — but not verified hours, type ratings, or career outcomes. Is the PRP a "LinkedIn for pilots"?
66. AOPA, EAA have member resources — but they're membership organizations, not career platforms. Am I competing with them?
67. AirlinePilotCentral has salary data — but not lifestyle data or career pathways. Am I the "lifestyle + career" version?
68. The app should be the "Glassdoor + LinkedIn + Logbook + Exam Prep for pilots" — is that too broad? Should I focus?
69. The app's uniqueness is HONESTY — no one else says "maybe don't become a pilot." Is that a brand or a death sentence?
70. The app should study its competitors — but more importantly, it should study what they DON'T do. That's the gap. What is it?

98. The app should be the answer to every persona in this directory — 23 personas, 2,300 questions, one app. Can one app answer all of them?
