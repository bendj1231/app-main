**The answer to Q100 — "If the app can't help me stop lying, can it at least help me stop needing to?"**

Yes. The lie exists because the truth is too painful to say: "I'm a 15-year CFI who wants to fly corporate jets and can't get there." The lie is a coping mechanism for the absence of a path.

When the app gives Neeraj a real path — the CFI-to-corporate transition guide, the hours quality analyzer, the corporate networking feature, the case studies of CFIs who escaped — the lie becomes unnecessary. You don't need to say "I love teaching" when you can say "I'm transitioning to corporate." You don't need to say "the 172 is my dream aircraft" when you can say "the G650 is my target, and here's my 18-month plan to get there."

The path IS the cure for the lie. Build the path, and the lie stops on its own. Not because Neeraj becomes brave, but because he no longer needs the lie to survive.

The app already has Neeraj as a named persona in the PSA ("15 years teaching. 7,000 hours. 3,000+ IFR simulator hours. 500+ students soloed. Stuck teaching not by choice — because leaving means unemployment"). The app already says his situation is a trap. What it doesn't say is: "Here's the way out." The Anonymous "I Want Out" feature and the CFI-to-Corporate Transition Guide are the two products that turn the app's documentation of Neeraj's prison into the key that unlocks it.

Build those, and Captain Neeraj stops being the PSA's tragic figure. He becomes the app's first success story: "15-year CFI, 4,000 hours, sim pilot, secret dreamer — and now flying a Citation Latitude because the platform gave him the path."
