**Highest-priority 200-hour low-timer builds (ranked):**

1. **Hour-Building Job Board + Pathway Pages** (Free) — the app has ZERO content on banner tow, skydiving, survey, pipeline patrol. These are the actual jobs 200hr pilots need. This is the #1 gap in the entire platform. Q11–Q80.

2. **Non-CFI Hour-Building Options Guide** ($19) — every CFI catalog I built assumes CFI is the path. But Q11 is "I don't want to teach." This guide is the single most important product for pilots who need alternatives. Q11.

3. **Hour-Building Complete Program** ($399) — the Foundation Program ($49) is mentorship-focused. The Transition Program ($299) is airline-focused. There is NO structured program for the 200→1,500 hour gap itself. This is the missing product. Q1–Q100.

4. **"200 Hours, Broke, Willing to Go Anywhere" Action Plan Session** ($129) — the single highest-value consultation in the entire catalog. This is the moment of maximum despair and maximum need. Q100.

5. **Hour Tracker Pro (200→1,500)** ($9/mo) — the logbook exists but doesn't project toward 1,500hrs or flag quality vs pattern work hours. This is the daily-use tool that keeps pilots engaged through the gap. Q1, Q14.

6. **"Valley of Death" Relief Fund** (charitable) — directly addresses the app's core PSA mission. The shortage content says "200-hour graduates with no placement." This fund is the actionable response. Q3, Q9, Q12.

7. **Operator Talent Pipeline (B2B)** — the enterprise infra exists ($1,000/yr). Extending it to hour-building operators (banner tow, survey, cargo, charter) creates the supply-demand bridge the PSA content demands. Q11–Q80.

This stage is the app's **reason for existing**. The Pilot Gap Module, the shortage PSA, the Airbus quote ("200-hour pilot is qualified"), the ALPA counter-position, the Sunk Cost Trap, the AOM Trap — all of this content identifies the problem but doesn't solve it with products. The Hour-Building Job Board and the Non-CFI Options Guide are the two products that turn the app's advocacy into actionable solutions. Without them, the app is documenting the problem; with them, it's solving it.
