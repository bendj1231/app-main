# Writing Style Guide: Government White Paper Neutrality

## Core Principle

All prose in the UCF official release reflects a **Swiss-neutral, government white paper register**. The document describes structural conditions within an industry. It does not assign blame, advocate for a position, or editorialize. It does not speak in the first person.

---

## Voice & Perspective

- **Third-person throughout.** No "we", "our", "you", or "us".
- **Passive or impersonal constructions preferred.** The subject is the system, the gap, the framework — not the company.
- **No rhetorical flourish.** No punchy one-liners, no climactic closing sentences, no dramatic reversals.
- **No sales language.** Avoid: "transform", "revolutionize", "unlock", "empower", "game-changing", "cutting-edge".

---

## Framing: Problems Are Structural, Not Attributable

- Problems are described as **systemic conditions**, not failures of specific actors.
- No individual platform, operator, regulator, or stakeholder is identified as the cause of a problem.
- Use framing such as:
  - *"This represents a structural gap in the industry's credentialling infrastructure rather than a deficiency attributable to any individual platform or stakeholder."*
  - *"Each platform operates within a closed, proprietary infrastructure designed for its primary operational purpose."*
  - *"No cross-platform verification layer currently exists."*
- Avoid framing such as:
  - *"The industry cannot see it."* — accusatory
  - *"The industry simply lacks the mechanism."* — implied criticism
  - *"The data exists. The industry has no mechanism to access it."* — blaming

---

## Framing: Solutions Are Additive, Not Disruptive

- The platform is described as **complementary** to existing providers, never as a replacement or disruptor.
- Use framing such as:
  - *"The platform is designed to be additive to existing provider relationships — no substitution of service, no displacement of user base."*
  - *"PilotRecognition operates in a complementary capacity to all providers in this category."*
  - *"Its function is structural: to receive, verify, and credential the data these platforms already generate."*
- Avoid framing such as:
  - *"No provider loses a user."* — defensive
  - *"We built this to fix what was broken."* — self-congratulatory

---

## Sentence Construction

| Avoid | Prefer |
|---|---|
| "The industry cannot see it." | "The operational differentiation is captured at platform level but is not accessible through existing credentialling pathways." |
| "We transform fragmented telemetry into credentials." | "Fragmented platform telemetry is ingested, verified against institutional sources, and consolidated into a portable credential." |
| "Airlines have no way to access this data." | "No structured mechanism currently exists for the transmission of this data to operator assessment processes." |
| "The pilot retains full control." | "Each integration is governed by an explicit, pilot-issued authorisation, scoped to read-only access." |
| "Zero professional credit." | "No formal professional recognition for that activity is currently available within existing industry frameworks." |

---

## Tone Benchmarks

Reference the following approved passages as style anchors:

> *"This represents a structural gap in the industry's credentialling infrastructure rather than a deficiency attributable to any individual platform or stakeholder."*

> *"Each platform operates within a closed, proprietary infrastructure designed for its primary operational purpose. As a consequence, the professional data generated within these environments has no structured pathway into a pilot's career record."*

> *"The platform is designed to be additive to existing provider relationships — no substitution of service, no displacement of user base."*

> *"The absence of a structured mechanism linking simulator telemetry to competency evidence represents a documented gap in the professional credentialling infrastructure."*

---

## Checklist Before Publishing Any Section

- [ ] No first-person pronouns ("we", "our", "us")
- [ ] No blame attributed to any named or unnamed party
- [ ] No sales or marketing language
- [ ] No rhetorical one-liners or punchy closing sentences
- [ ] Problems framed as structural conditions, not failures
- [ ] Solutions framed as additive infrastructure, not disruption
- [ ] Passive or impersonal constructions used where possible
