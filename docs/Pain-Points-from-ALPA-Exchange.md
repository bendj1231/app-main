# 10 Pain Points from the ALPA-PSA Exchange

*Compiled from the simulated message-stress-test between ALPA and PSA positions.*  
*These are real industry problems surfaced through adversarial argumentation.*

---

## 1. The CFI Wage Trap

**The Problem:** Certified Flight Instructors earn $30,000–$35,000/year while building the 1,500 hours required for an ATP. This is below a living wage in most U.S. markets, forcing CFIs into debt spirils, side jobs, or career abandonment.

**Who Owns It:** Flight schools (ATOs) set CFI pay. ALPA does not represent CFIs (Part 61/141, not Part 121). PSA advocates for them but does not sign paychecks.

**The Debate:** ALPA says "not our jurisdiction." PSA says "if you claim to protect the next generation, you cannot ignore the people training them."

---

## 2. The 2–4 Year Instructor Queue

**The Problem:** The gap between graduating flight school (200 hours) and reaching airline hireable status (1,500+ hours) is not a gap — it is a years-long purgatory. The instructor position queue is backed up because senior instructors cannot advance, blocking junior ones from getting instructing jobs at all.

**Who Owns It:** Airlines control hiring. Flight schools control instructor slots. ALPA controls seniority rules that prevent experienced instructors from bypassing the queue.

**The Debate:** ALPA frames it as "standard incubation" (like medical residency). PSA frames it as a structural trap where the queue itself becomes the barrier.

---

## 3. Predatory Flight School Marketing

**The Problem:** Flight schools charge $50,000–$100,000+ upfront, often financed through private loans, with no disclosure of actual placement rates, time-to-hire, or the reality of the instructor queue. Graduates enroll believing an airline career is imminent.

**Who Owns It:** ATOs write the brochures. The FAA does not require placement-rate disclosure. ALPA explicitly stated it is "not a consumer protection agency."

**The Debate:** ALPA blames the ATOs. PSA demands transparency partnerships with schools willing to publish real data.

---

## 4. The Seniority Trap / Non-Portable Experience

**The Problem:** A pilot with 6,000 hours of instruction, multiple ratings, and 15 years of experience who moves to a new airline starts at the bottom of the seniority list — behind a 23-year-old with 1,500 hours and a fresh ATP. Their capabilities do not travel. Only their clock resets.

**Who Owns It:** ALPA negotiates every seniority list and has consistently rejected portability proposals.

**The Debate:** ALPA calls seniority "democratic protection against corporate favoritism." PSA calls it "indentured servitude with better branding."

---

## 5. The Regional-to-Mainline Chasm

**The Problem:** Regional airline pilots are trapped in a two-tier system. They do the same job (flying commercial passengers) for 30–50% of mainline pay, with worse schedules, worse bases, and no real pathway to mainline except "flow agreements" that rarely flow on schedule.

**Who Owns It:** ALPA negotiated the scope clauses that allowed mainline airlines to outsource flying to regionals. ALPA also negotiated the regional contracts — including the decades of low wages.

**The Debate:** ALPA claims historic post-COVID raises. PSA points out those raises only came after a labor shortage forced management's hand, and regional QOL remains abysmal.

---

## 6. The 95% Visibility Problem

**The Problem:** ALPA represents 78,000+ active Part 121 pilots. The civilian pipeline produces 8,000–10,000 new CPLs annually, but airlines hire only 4,000–5,000. The "excess" pilots — the ones who never get hired, who age out, who quit aviation — are invisible to the union that claims to protect the profession.

**Who Owns It:** ALPA defines its constituency as active airline pilots. The unemployed, the CFIs, the quitters are not members and therefore not represented.

**The Debate:** ALPA says "record ATP issuances prove the pipeline works." PSA says "issuing certificates is not the same as hiring pilots."

---

## 7. The "Appropriate Placement" Euphemism

**The Problem:** ALPA's contracts allow direct-entry hiring of experienced pilots — but only at "appropriate seniority placement," which universally means the bottom. This is presented as fairness but functions as a punitive reset that erases experience.

**Who Owns It:** ALPA attorneys draft the seniority language in every contract.

**The Debate:** ALPA says "existing pilots earned their positions." PSA says "a 6,000-hour instructor did not earn the bottom — they earned a fair credit for their experience."

---

## 8. The Displacement Hypocrisy

**The Problem:** ALPA argues that portable experience would "displace existing pilots" (worse base, delayed Captain upgrade). Yet ALPA has negotiated every airline merger that staples one seniority list to the bottom of another — explicitly displacing thousands of pilots.

**Who Owns It:** ALPA negotiated the US Airways/America West stapling, the Delta/Northwest integration, the Republic/Shuttle America merger, and dozens more.

**The Debate:** ALPA says "we protected those pilots with legal battles." PSA says "you created the displacement you now claim to protect against."

---

## 9. The Gatekeeper Mentality

**The Problem:** ALPA's reflex when challenged is to end the discussion, dismiss critics as "corporate fronts," and diagnose dissent as pathology. This reveals an organization that has become more interested in protecting its walls than fixing its leaks.

**The Evidence:** ALPA ended the discussion three times ("This discussion is over," "The record stands," "Our door is permanently shut"). ALPA called PSA a "corporate front" without naming a single donor. ALPA diagnosed unemployed pilots as "failing background checks" without evidence.

**The Debate:** ALPA says "we protect the profession from corporate exploitation." PSA says "you protect the profession from criticism."

---

## 10. The Conflation of Safety and Seniority

**The Problem:** ALPA's defense of every pipeline criticism is to invoke safety (the 1,500-hour rule, Colgan 3407, the 99.8% fatality drop). But the 1,500-hour rule addresses cockpit entry standards. It does not address the predatory tuition, the CFI wage trap, the non-portable seniority, or the opaque hiring. By conflating the two, ALPA makes any pipeline reform sound like an attack on safety.

**Who Owns It:** ALPA's messaging strategy intentionally blurs the line between "safety standards" and "labor protection."

**The Debate:** ALPA says "you cannot separate the hours from the ecosystem." PSA says "the ecosystem has a leak, and calling it 'safety' does not fix it."
