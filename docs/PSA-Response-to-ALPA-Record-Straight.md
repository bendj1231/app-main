# PSA → ALPA: Response to "Setting the Record Straight"

**From:** Karl Brian Vogt, Director, WM Pilot Group; Founder, pilotshortage.org / Wingman Network  
**To:** Capt. Jason Ambrosi, President, Air Line Pilots Association  
**Date:** June 1, 2026  
**Subject:** RE: Setting the Record Straight: Professional Standards vs. The Devaluation of Aviation Safety

---

Dear Captain Ambrosi,

Thank you for your direct and candid response. I welcome the clarity it provides. Your letter codifies, in writing, the exact structural reality that the Wingman Network (and the broader aviation community via pilotshortage.org) has been trying to bring to light.

You stated that ALPA makes no apologies for being a fiercely focused, democratically elected labor union whose mandate is strictly to protect its dues-paying members. I respect that. A union's job is to protect its backyard. However, by openly admitting that your focus stops at the border of your membership, you confirm our core thesis: ALPA is an elite insider club, and the thousands of low-hour pilots outside your wall are entirely on their own.

Let us look past the rhetoric and address the economic and systemic realities you raised.

---

## 1. The Weaponization of the 1,500-Hour Rule

Your letter contains a striking admission. You explicitly frame the 1,500-hour rule not just as a safety shield, but as an economic supply-control weapon designed to make "real-world experience scarce and highly valuable."

While you credit this scarcity with forcing regional airlines to raise wages post-COVID, you completely ignore who pays the price for that artificial scarcity: the entry-level aviator.

To secure those 1,500 hours, a young pilot must take on upwards of $100,000 in high-interest debt, only to be funneled into a highly exploitative Certified Flight Instructor (CFI) bottleneck earning $30,000 a year. ALPA champions the $100,000 starting salary at the end of the tunnel, but completely dissociates itself from the financial meat-grinder young aviators must survive to get there. Using a safety regulation as an economic lever to protect senior contracts at the expense of entry-level poverty is not labor solidarity; it is a structural gate.

---

## 2. The Dehumanization of Stagnant Careers

Your characterization of a pilot stuck in the flight-instruction loop for a decade as an "individual operational outcome" rather than a systemic failure is perhaps the most revealing part of your message.

To chalk up a decade-long career stall purely to personal or competency failure is diagnostic overreach that flies in the face of recent history. A pilot who graduated in 2015 did not operate in a vacuum. They faced the regional airline hiring freezes of 2016, the total operational collapse and massive furloughs of COVID-19 in 2020, and the current 2025–2026 regional contractions caused by global aircraft delivery delays.

When ALPA dismisses these stranded professionals as mere "operational anomalies," you prove our point: When the system breaks, ALPA blames the individual so it does not have to audit the pipeline.

---

## 3. The "Corporate Interest" Straw Man

You repeatedly attempt to delegitimize our advocacy by painting us as "corporate interests looking for cheap labor." Let me be completely clear: pilotshortage.org and the Wingman Network are not funded by airline executives, nor are we lobbying to lower safety standards or repeal the 1,500-hour rule.

Our published proposals demand:
- Standardized, transparent reporting of flight school placement rates.
- Clear, data-driven visibility into airline hiring requirements.
- Portable, merit-based recognition of demonstrated competency for CFIs.

None of these initiatives lower the bar. They simply bring transparency to a pipeline that is currently opaque, volatile, and heavily weighted against the applicant. Labeling a call for data and student transparency as a "threat to public safety" is a classic defensive tactic used to avoid engaging with the substance of the critique.

---

## The Way Forward

The aviation ecosystem is a delicate pipeline. ALPA has successfully built a fortress for the 78,000 pilots inside the gate. But a fortress that ignores the health of its supply line will eventually starve from the bottom up.

If ALPA truly cares about the next generation of aviators — beyond university marketing clubs and aspirational lobbying — it must stop treating non-members as collateral damage or corporate tools.

We do not want to tear down your wall, Captain Ambrosi. We want an honest, data-driven accounting of what it costs a human being to climb over it.

Sincerely,

**Karl Brian Vogt**
