ALPA has consistently engaged with your organization in good faith to clarify the legal, economic, and operational structures that govern commercial aviation labor. However, your final rebuttal makes it clear that your objective is not a constructive dialogue on industry safety or pilot advancement, but rather a fundamental ideological dispute with the statutory definition of organized labor itself.

Because this exchange has reached a point of circular rhetoric, ALPA offers this final, definitive response to clarify our position for the record.

---

## 1. The Core Purpose of a Labor Union

You characterize our statement—"We are a collective bargaining agent. We protect our members"—as a "surrender." To the 78,000 airline pilots who democratically elect our leadership and fund our operations, that statement is our solemn oath.

A labor union is not a general trade association, a political party, or a public charity. It is a legally certified representative under federal law. Criticizing ALPA for prioritizing its dues-paying members is equivalent to criticizing a defense attorney for prioritizing their client, or a corporation for prioritizing its shareholders. It does not represent a moral failure; it represents the precise execution of a legal and fiduciary mandate.

---

## 2. The Fallacy of the "Graveyard of Abandoned Careers"

Your comparison of flight instruction to medical residencies or law clerkships relies on an engineered narrative of failure. Every highly compensated, highly regulated profession in the world requires individuals to bear the initial risk of training, certification, and medical eligibility.

- A law student can fail the bar exam or fail to secure a firm placement.
- A pre-med student can fail to match into a residency.
- A student pilot faces the reality that aviation requires stringent medical, cognitive, and practical standards.

The aviation industry does not guarantee an airline career to anyone simply because they bought a logbook and a headset. The high safety margins of commercial aviation exist precisely because the pipeline filters for performance, discipline, and medical fitness. ALPA's role is to ensure that for those who successfully meet these uncompromising standards, a stable, lifelong, and lucrative profession exists on the other side.

---

## 3. Safety and Scarcity Are Inseparable Economics

Your rebuttal attempts to create a contradiction by stating that ALPA uses the 1,500-hour rule for "labor market control" rather than safety. This displays a fundamental misunderstanding of labor economics.

In a capitalist economy, professional value is intrinsically linked to qualification standards. The 1,500-hour rule is, first and foremost, a safety standard mandated by Congress following the tragic crash of Colgan Air Flight 3407. It ensures that no pilot commands a commercial airliner without adequate real-world operational experience.

The economic byproduct—that high standards create qualified scarcity, which in turn drives up wages—is a feature of every highly skilled profession, from specialized surgeons to structural engineers. ALPA makes no apologies for defending a safety standard that simultaneously prevents passenger fatalities and protects the economic dignity of the piloting profession.

---

## 4. The Answer to Your Final Question

You ask what ALPA's plan is for an individual who has been instructing for over a decade without transitioning to an airline.

The honest, professional answer is that an individual's career progression depends on a multitude of personal and professional variables: their check-ride record, their medical status, their geographic mobility, their interview performance, and their background checks. If an individual has been unable to secure a position at a single Part 121 or Part 135 carrier during the most aggressive, historic hiring boom in the history of aviation (2021–2025), that is an individual operational outcome, not a systemic failure of the union.

---

## Conclusion

ALPA will not apologize for the historic contracts we have secured, the retirement funds we protect, or the safety regulations we defend. We have built the most stable, lucrative, and safe commercial aviation ecosystem in human history.

Every Certified Flight Instructor working today benefits directly from the high wage floors, regional airline stability, and career longevity that ALPA fought for and won. We will continue to focus 100% of our resources on defending those standards for our current members and for the next generation of aviators who successfully earn their place on a master seniority list.

This concludes our correspondence on this matter. We wish you the best in your advocacy for general aviation businesses.

Sincerely,
