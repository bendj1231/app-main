# ALPA vs. Pilot Shortage Association — The Complete Exchange

**Date Range:** June 1, 2026
**Parties:** Capt. Jason Ambrosi, President, Air Line Pilots Association (ALPA) and The Pilot Shortage Association (PSA)
**Topic:** The inverted pipeline, the 1,500-hour rule, seniority systems, and who speaks for the 95% of pilots outside the airline flight deck.

---

## Opening Salvo: ALPA

**From:** Capt. Jason Ambrosi, President, Air Line Pilots Association (ALPA)  
**To:** The Executive Committee, Pilot Shortage Association (PSA)  
**Subject:** RE: Your "Industry Coalition" Campaign and Inverted Pipeline Narrative

> Let us strip away the theater and look at the foundation of your argument. You accuse ALPA of building a seniority "trap" that keeps experience captive, and you demand we "prove our commitment" by dismantling the very framework that turned a chaotic, exploitative trade into a stable, middle-class profession.

> Your letter is a textbook corporate attempt to blame labor for the structural flaws of a market you built and you profit from.

**Key Arguments:**
1. **Seniority protects against corporate favoritism** — portability would create a race to the bottom where management pits pilots against each other.
2. **Flight schools (not ALPA) exploit CFIs** — ALPA is a Part 121 union; ATOs paying $30k is not ALPA's fault.
3. **The 1,500-hour rule is foundational safety** — part of the 2010 Act that overhauled fatigue rules and created the PRD. 99.8% fatality drop.
4. **EBT/CBTA are complementary, not replacements** — they improve simulator training; they do not replace real-world decision-making.
5. **Regional airlines ruined QOL, not ALPA** — Captain retention crisis is corporate, not union-made.
6. **ALPA's invitation:** Hire 6,000-hour instructors directly into mainline via direct-entry mechanisms. Start at appropriate seniority placement. Pay industry-standard rates.

## Response 1: PSA

**From:** The Pilot Shortage Association  
**To:** Capt. Jason Ambrosi, ALPA  
**Subject:** The Pilot Gap Is Real

> You almost had us. Then you wrote the sentence that undoes your entire letter.

> *"ALPA's contracts allow airlines to hire direct-entry First Officers at any time, provided they start at the appropriate seniority placement."*

> There it is. The trap, fully revealed. "Appropriate seniority placement" means the bottom.

**Key Counter-Arguments:**
1. **The seniority system is a caste system** — A Captain with 12 years at a bankrupt regional starts over as an FO. Capabilities do not travel. Only seniority dies.
2. **ALPA wrote the contracts** — You negotiated every word. Do not blame management for a system ALPA built.
3. **The 2010 Act fixed the cockpit, not the pipeline** — No requirement for airlines to publish real hiring requirements. No requirement for flight schools to disclose placement rates.
4. **ALPA ignores CFIs because they are not Part 121** — Where is ALPA's CFI union? Why has ALPA never organized them?
5. **Regional QOL is abysmal because ALPA accepted it** — ALPA negotiated the two-tier system, the flow gimmicks, the scope clauses.
6. **The real divide:** ALPA's loyalty is to incumbent members who already made it through the gate. Everyone else is invisible.

## Response 2: PSA

**Subject:** The Record Stands

4. **The 2010 Act did not fix the pipeline** — No disclosure requirements for airlines or flight schools. No pathway for non-military CPL holders.
5. **Seniority already displaces** — Every airline merger staples junior lists to the bottom.
6. **ALPA cannot name a single corporate donor to PSA** — The accusation is projection.

> ALPA holds the winning tickets. We hold the receipts.

**To:** The Executive Committee, Pilot Shortage Association (PSA)  
**Subject:** CLOSING RECORD: The Protection of the Profession is Non-Negotiable

> Since you have declared this your final word, let us leave the industry, our members, and the next generation of aviators with the cold, unyielding facts of the profession.

**Key Arguments:**
1. **Regional compensation math** — $90–$100/hr × 75 guaranteed hours × 12 months = $81,000–$90,000 base. Add per diem, uniform allowances, 401(k) (up to 15%), and $20,000–$50,000 signing bonuses.
2. **Career trajectory** — Regional FO → Mainline Captain = $350,000–$450,000+. A truck driver hits a ceiling immediately. Pilots have a multi-million-dollar arc.
3. **The 95% is a manufactured myth** — Record ATP issuances. The gap between CPL and airline hire is standard incubation (like medical residency). Framing it as "corporate failure" insults every pilot who earned their stripes.
4. **The 1,500-hour rule is literal safety foundation** — Ensures pilots have encountered real icing, real crosswinds, real mechanical anomalies as PIC before carrying 150 passengers.
5. **Seniority is democratic** — Without it, executives bypass collective bargaining and play favorites. Existing pilots earned their positions through time and dedication.
6. **PSA's "separate track" would displace existing pilots** — Dropping a 6,000-hour external pilot into the middle of a seniority list explicitly pushes existing pilots out of their base, forces commuting, and delays upgrade.

> ALPA will continue to stand at the gateway of this profession, defending the safety standards passed by Congress and the historic contracts earned by our members.

5. **ALPA's shield, protection, and record** — Aeromedical teams protect certificates. Attorneys spend millions protecting pilots from merger stapling. ALPA "broke the corporate model" by restricting cheap labor.

## Impact & Context

This exchange represents the first direct confrontation between ALPA — the world's largest airline pilot union, representing 78,000+ Part 121 aviators — and PSA, a grassroots advocacy group speaking for the estimated 95% of qualified pilots who never reach an airline flight deck.

The debate crystallizes two irreconcilable worldviews:

- **ALPA** sees aviation as a profession protected by walls: seniority, hours, union contracts. Those walls keep corporate exploitation out. Anyone outside is either waiting their turn or unfit to enter.

- **PSA** sees aviation as a pipeline with a leak: flight schools produce more CPLs than airlines hire, the 1,500-hour queue is a financial death sentence for many, and the seniority system traps experienced aviators in place. The walls protect the insiders. Everyone else pays the price.

The exchange has been archived at pilotshortage.org and is now part of the permanent public record on the state of the civilian aviation pipeline in the United States.
